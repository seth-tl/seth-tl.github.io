My research focuses on developing mathematical and computational methods for understanding, approximating, and predicting complex dynamical systems, with particular emphasis on preserving their underlying geometric and physical structure. This work has involved three interrelated programs of research.

Computational Geometric Hydrodynamics

The geometric formulation of continuum mechanics provides a powerful framework for understanding the structure of fluid and plasma dynamics. My research develops numerical methods that leverage this structure, particularly through the theory of Lie groups, geometric integration, and Riemannian geometry. I am especially interested in the interplay between geometry, hydrodynamics, information theory, and optimal transport along with their use in the formulation of structure-preserving algorithms. This includes the development of information-geometric regularization methods, Lie group integration schemes on spaces of diffeomorphisms and symplectic transformations, and the geometric analysis of these methods.

Data-Driven Dynamics

Data-driven dynamical modelling has become a contemporary focus of computational mathematics. These methods present a fascinating new approaches to the modelling of complex physical systems combining theory and observations. My research investigates how ideas from numerical analysis, approximation theory, and geometric mechanics can be incorporated into system identification and prediction in this context. A central focus is the development of reliable methods for learning nonlinear evolution operators, including kernel-based approximation, high-order system identification, and geometric operator learning. I am particularly interested in methods that respect the geometry of the underlying state space and remain accurate and stable over long prediction horizons.

Numerical Methods for Atmospheric Dynamics

My research explores geometric numerical methods for geophysical fluid dynamics, with applications to atmospheric transport, numerical weather prediction, and hybrid partial differential equation models. I am particularly interested in developing computational methods for dynamical cores and combining physics-based numerical models with data-driven prediction. These directions include geometric operator learning for atmospheric dynamics and the representation of atmospheric states using tools from shape analysis.
