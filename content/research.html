My research focuses on developing mathematical and computational methods for understanding, approximating, and predicting complex dynamical systems, with particular emphasis on preserving their underlying geometric and physical structure. This work has involved three interrelated programs of research.

Computational Geometric Hydrodynamics

The geometric formulation of continuum mechanics provides a powerful framework for understanding the structure of fluid and plasma dynamics. My research develops numerical methods that exploit this structure, particularly through the theory of Lie groups, geometric integration, and characteristic mapping methods. I am especially interested in the interplay between geometry, information theory, and optimal transport in the formulation of structure-preserving numerical algorithms. This includes the development of information-geometric regularization methods, thermodynamically constrained fluid models, and numerical representations of diffeomorphisms and symplectic transformations.

Data-Driven Dynamics

Data-driven modeling offers new approaches to learning the evolution of complex dynamical systems directly from observations. My research investigates how ideas from numerical analysis, approximation theory, and geometric mechanics can be incorporated into system identification and machine learning. A central focus is the development of reliable methods for learning nonlinear evolution operators, including kernel-based approximation, high-order system identification, and geometric operator learning. I am particularly interested in methods that respect the geometry of the underlying state space and remain accurate and stable over long prediction horizons.

Numerical Methods in Earth Sciences

Mathematical models of the Earth system present challenging numerical problems involving nonlinear dynamics, multiscale interactions, and complex geometries. My research explores geometric numerical methods for geophysical fluid dynamics, with applications to atmospheric transport, numerical weather prediction, and environmental modeling.

I am particularly interested in developing computational methods for dynamical cores and combining physics-based numerical models with data-driven prediction. These directions include geometric operator learning for atmospheric dynamics and the representation of atmospheric states using tools from shape analysis.
