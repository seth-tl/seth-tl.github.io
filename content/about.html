I'm Seth Taylor, PIMS postdoctoral fellow at the University of Saskatchewan in the Department of Computer Science in Raymond Spiteri's group. My research is supported by the PIMS-CRG on structure-preserving discretizations and their applications, Environment and Climate Change Canada, and NSERC through their Alliance Advantage program. My work lies at the intersection of computational mathematics, numerical analysis, and geometric mechanics. I am seeking to bridge insights and intuition coming from geometry for the construction of numerical methods for the simulation of complex physical phenomena. My particular interests include infinite-dimensional geometry, structure-preserving discretizations, continuum mechanics, numerical methods for PDEs, and data-driven dynamics.

I earned my Ph.D. in the Department of Mathematics and Statistics at McGill University under the supervision of Jean-Christophe Nave. My doctoral research was supported by an NSERC CGS-D grant and focused on characteristic mapping methods along with their generalizations as functional Lie group integrators. In this work, I developed a geometric analysis for these methods, numerical schemes for their application to geophysical PDE models, and structure-preserving geometric operator learning techniques. My current projects are related to hybrid numerical weather prediction, structure-preserving methods for plasma physics, high-order approximaations for data-driven dynamics, and information geometric regularizations of compressible fluids.
