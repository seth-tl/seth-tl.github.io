I'm Seth Taylor, PIMS postdoctoral fellow at the University of Saskatchewan in the Department of Computer Science in Raymond Spiteri's group. My research is supported by the PIMS-CRG on structure-preserving discretizations and their applications, Environment and Climate Change Canada, and NSERC through their Alliance Advantage program. My work lies at the intersection of computational mathematics, numerical analysis, and geometric mechanics. I am seeking to bridge insights from infinite-dimensional geometry for the numerical simulation of complex physical phenomena.

I earned my Ph.D. in the Department of Mathematics and Statistics at McGill University under the supervision of Jean-Christophe Nave. This work was supported by an NSERC CGS-D grant and focused on the development of characteristic mapping methods and their formalization as functional Lie group integrators. My work involved the geometric analysis of these methods, numerical schemes for their application to geophysical PDE models, and the construction of geometric operator learning techniques.

My current projects are related to hybrid numerical weather prediction, structure-preserving methods for plasma physics, high-order approximations for data-driven dynamics, and information geometric regularizations of compressible fluids.
