I'm Seth Taylor, PIMS postdoctoral fellow at the University of Saskatchewan in the Department of Computer Science. My research lies at the intersection of computational mathematics, numerical analysis, and geometric mechanics. In this work I seek to bridge insights and intuition coming from geometry towards the development of novel numerical methods for the simulation of complex physical phenomena. My particular interests include structure-preserving discretizations, geometric modelling approaches in continuum mechanics, numerical methods for nonlinear partial differential equations, and data-driven dynamics.

I earned my Ph.D. in the Department of Mathematics and Statistics at McGill University under the supervision of Jean-Christophe Nave. My doctoral research focused on characteristic mapping methods and their generalizations as functional Lie group integrators. I applied these techniques to geophysical PDE models and in the context of geometric operator learning. My current interests include hybrid numerical weather prediction, data-driven dynamics, and connections between geometry, information theory, and scientific computing.
