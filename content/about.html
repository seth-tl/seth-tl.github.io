Hello! I'm Seth Taylor, PIMS postdoctoral fellow at the University of Saskatchewan in the Department of Computer Science within Raymond Spiteri's group. My research is supported by a PIMS-CRG on structure-preserving discretizations and their applications, Environment and Climate Change Canada, and NSERC through their Alliance Advantage program. I earned my Ph.D. in the Department of Mathematics and Statistics at McGill University under the supervision of Jean-Christophe Nave. This work was supported by an NSERC CGS-D grant and focused on the development of characteristic mapping methods for geophysical PDEs, geometric operator learning methods for turbulent fluid flows, and the analysis of some infinite-dimensional extensions of Lie group integrators. My experstise lies at the intersection of computational mathematics, numerical analysis, and geometric mechanics. The overarching goal of my research is to bridge insights from infinite-dimensional geometry towards the design and analysis of numerical methods for the simulation of complex physical phenomena.

My current projects are related to the topics of hybrid numerical weather prediction, structure-preserving methods for plasma physics, high-order approximations for data-driven dynamics, and information geometric regularizations of compressible fluids. Please feel free to reach out if you have any questions about this work.
